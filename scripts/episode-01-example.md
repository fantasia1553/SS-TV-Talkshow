# Episode 01 — Example Script

**v0.1 · 英文对白 / 中文提示 · 可修改示例**  
**题目：** Living Together: How Can Locals and Newcomers Build a Sense of Belonging in Singapore?  
**角色：** H 主持人；L 本地学生角色；N 新来新加坡的学生角色；R 学生资料员。演员均待填写。  
**对应：** [大纲](../show-outline/episode-01.md) · [来源记录](../research-sources/source-log.md)

> 以下学生经历是编写的课堂情景，不代表组员、任何国籍群体或真实受访者。对白中的建议也不是已获学校批准的安排。方括号是制作提示，不念出。A–G 对应 8 分钟编排框架，实际时长须排练确认。

## A. Opening

[四人就座；展示标题。]

**H:** Welcome to SS-TV-Talkshow. Today's question is simple: can we share a classroom without really feeling that we belong? We are looking at cultural and nationality diversity in Singapore, especially how local students and students new to Singapore can connect.

**H:** Our student stories today are fictional examples. They are here to start a discussion, not to represent everyone's experience. Let's begin with a group project.

## B. Two perspectives

**H:** L, imagine a new student joins your class. What might make it difficult to include them?

**L:** In this example, I already have friends I usually work with. When a project starts, I turn to them automatically. I might not notice that someone else is still looking for a group.

**N:** From my side, I might want to join but feel unsure about interrupting. If everyone seems to know one another, I could think the group is already full.

**H:** So one person sees a familiar routine, while another sees a closed door?

**L:** Yes. But I also wouldn't want to pressure someone. How do I know whether they want to join?

**N:** You could ask, “Would you like to work with us?” That gives me a choice. I can also take a first step by asking whether there is a task I can help with.

**H:** What about language or jokes that not everyone understands?

**N:** I might ask what something means. It would help if someone explained it without making me feel silly.

**L:** And I could check that everyone understands the task before we move on. That seems useful for the whole group.

## C. Evidence check

[显示资料卡 S01：MCCY / National Integration Council；屏幕附来源链接或参考页编号。]

**H:** R, how does this connect with the material you checked?

**R:** The National Integration Council describes integration as a two-way and continuing process. Newcomers can learn about local norms, and locals can help them understand and participate. That is a policy principle, not proof that any particular classroom activity will work. [S01]

**H:** So we should explain what the source supports, and what we are suggesting ourselves.

**R:** Exactly. Our classroom story is fictional. Our next idea is a proposal that would need feedback.

## D. Proposal and challenge

**H:** What could our class try?

**L:** We could suggest an optional buddy activity for a shared task. Instead of just saying hello, students could plan something together, such as a short class presentation.

**N:** I like having something specific to do. But I wouldn't want to be introduced only as “the foreign student”. I would want people to know my interests too.

**H:** Could a buddy system feel forced?

**N:** Yes. People should be able to choose whether to join, and ask to change partners. It should not feel like a test of how well they fit in.

**L:** There is another issue. If the same students always explain everything, they might feel responsible for everyone's problems.

**R:** Then our proposal could rotate tasks and share responsibility. The teacher could help with problems that students cannot solve. We should ask whether the workload is fair.

**H:** How would we know if it helped?

**R:** We could invite anonymous feedback about whether students felt able to contribute. We could also ask if they wanted to work together again. These are possible checks, not results we already have.

## E. A community connection

[显示资料卡 S02：People's Association / Community Integration。]

**H:** Is there a real example beyond our fictional classroom?

**R:** The People's Association lists community volunteering and festive celebrations among its integration activities. Those are examples of organised opportunities for interaction. The webpage alone does not establish how effective each activity is. [S02]

**L:** For our own class, a shared task sounds more manageable than expecting people to become close friends immediately.

**N:** And the task should let both people contribute. I might be new to the school, but I can still have ideas or skills to share.

## F. Audience question

**H:** Imagine someone joins your project group tomorrow. What is one action that would help them contribute, without putting them on the spot?

[现场版：留 15–20 秒思考，邀请一至两位自愿回答；不要求公开个人移民经历。]

**H:** Thank you. Which part of that action gives the new member a real choice?

[主持人根据实际回答追问；无回应时改读下句。录播版可直接使用下句。]

**H:** One possible example is to explain the available tasks and ask which one they would like to take. We can discuss whether that would work in our class.

## G. Closing

**H:** Let's finish with one action from each of you.

**L:** I can make an invitation instead of assuming that someone wants to stay apart.

**N:** I can ask questions and share something I can contribute.

**R:** I can check our claims and listen to feedback before saying an idea is successful.

**H:** Our discussion suggests that belonging needs opportunities to participate, room for differences, and effort from more than one side. That is our proposed takeaway, and we welcome other views. Thank you for watching SS-TV-Talkshow.
